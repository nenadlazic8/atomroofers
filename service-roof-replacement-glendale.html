    Know The Signs

    Signs Your Glendale Roof Needs Replacing

    If you recognise two or more of these, a replacement is usually the cheaper decision over five years. Our free inspection tells you either way — and we will say so plainly if a repair is all you need.

    01

    The Roof Is 20+ Years Old

    Most asphalt shingle roofs in the San Fernando and Crescenta valleys reach the end of their useful life between 20 and 25 years. Heat exposure here accelerates it.

    02

    Curling, Cracked or Missing Shingles

    Shingles that cup, curl at the edges, or blow off in a wind event are no longer sealing. Cracked or slipping tile leaves the underlayment exposed to UV.

    03

    Leaks That Keep Coming Back

    A stain that returns in the same spot each rainy season means the failure is in the system, not the patch. Repeated repairs on one area signal a worn-out roof.

    04

    Sagging or Uneven Rooflines

    A dip along the ridge or a soft, spongy feel underfoot means moisture has reached the decking or rafters. This needs assessment quickly.

    05

    Granules Filling the Gutters

    Those dark sand-like granules are the shingle's UV shield. Once they wash into the gutters in volume, the asphalt underneath starts to dry out and crack.

    06

    Daylight Through the Attic

    If you can see light between the roof boards from inside the attic, or your cooling bills keep climbing, the roof and its ventilation are no longer doing their job.

    California Energy Code

    Cool Roofs and Title 24 in Glendale

    Replacing a roof in California is not just a material choice — it is a code event. Title 24, Part 6 of the California Energy Code sets the energy-efficiency rules a re-roof has to meet, and a tear-off down to the deck is exactly the kind of work that triggers them.

    A cool roof reflects more sunlight and holds less heat than a standard roof in the same colour. In practice that means a cooler attic, less load on the air conditioning through a Glendale summer, and a roof assembly that satisfies the prescriptive energy requirements without you having to reach for a performance workaround.

    Which requirement applies to your home depends on your roof's slope, the building type and Glendale's climate zone. We work that out from your address during the free inspection and tell you in writing which path your re-roof is taking — before you choose a material.

    Glendale's hillsides add a second rulebook Properties in a Very High Fire Hazard Severity Zone — which covers much of Glendale's canyon and foothill terrain — also fall under the wildland-urban interface provisions of the building code, where a Class A fire-rated assembly is the standard. Energy compliance and fire compliance are separate tests, and your roof has to pass both. We specify to whichever is stricter for your address.

    What compliance actually involves

    • The governing code Title 24, Part 6 — the California Energy Code. It is enforced at permit and at final inspection, not left to the contractor's discretion.
    • How products qualify Roofing products are rated by the Cool Roof Rating Council (CRRC) for solar reflectance and thermal emittance. The code references CRRC-listed values, so an unrated product cannot be used to show compliance.
    • Aged vs. initial values Compliance is judged on aged ratings, not the day-one numbers on the brochure — roofs get dirtier and less reflective over time, and the code accounts for it.
    • It is not only white roofs Cool-rated products exist across tile, standing-seam metal, architectural shingle and flat-roof membranes and coatings, in conventional colours. Meeting Title 24 does not force a white roof on you.
    • Where it is documented On the permit paperwork and the compliance forms filed with it. We prepare those and hand you the product documentation at closeout, so the file is complete if you sell.
    • If the prescriptive path does not suit A whole-building performance calculation can trade roof reflectance against other efficiency measures. It is more paperwork, and we will tell you plainly when it is and is not worth it.

    Permits & Inspections

    Who Permits a Roof Replacement at Your Address

    A roof replacement in the Glendale area is permitted and inspected by one of three different authorities, and which one depends entirely on your address — not on your mailing city or your zip code.

    Most of this page

    City of Glendale

    Building & Safety Division

    Properties inside Glendale city limits are permitted and inspected by the city's own Building & Safety Division. Not LADBS, and not LA County — Glendale is an incorporated city that runs its own building department, with its own counter, fees and inspection scheduling.

    Unincorporated areas

    Los Angeles County

    Public Works — Building & Safety

    Addresses in the unincorporated pockets around the Crescenta Valley are not in any city, so they fall to LA County. Different office, different fee schedule, different inspection process — even for two houses a few streets apart.

    City of LA addresses

    City of Los Angeles

    LADBS

    Neighbourhoods on the Los Angeles side of the line — Eagle Rock, Atwater Village, Highland Park, Tujunga, Sunland and Sun Valley among them — are permitted by LADBS, which has its own requirements and its own timelines.

    Why this matters more here than almost anywhere else The Glendale city line is unusually irregular. Mailing addresses, zip codes and neighbourhood names routinely cross it, which means a homeowner can genuinely not know which building department governs their roof — and a roofer who guesses wrong can end up with an unpermitted roof, a failed inspection, or a stalled escrow when the house sells. We confirm the jurisdiction from your address before we quote, pull the permit under our own licence, meet the inspector on site, and hand you the signed-off paperwork at the end. You do not visit a counter.

    Three kinds of Glendale roof

    Where your house sits in the city tells us most of what the job will involve before we are on the roof.

    Hillside & canyon

    Steep pitches, tight or single-access streets, and fire-zone requirements. These jobs need a Class A assembly, often a crane or conveyor rather than a ladder, and a tear-off plan that keeps debris off a slope.

    Typically Chevy Chase Canyon, Glenoaks Canyon, Verdugo Woodlands, Oakmont, College Hills, Whiting Woods and the Verdugo and San Rafael foothill streets.

    Older south Glendale

    South Glendale was partly the separate city of Tropico until Glendale annexed it in 1918, so the housing stock runs old. Expect original decking, spaced sheathing under tile, and layers that have to come off before anything new goes on.

    Typically Adams Hill and Adams Square, Vineyard, Citrus Grove, Pacific-Edison, Somerset and the Brand Boulevard side streets.

    Valley floor & multifamily

    Flatter lots, low-slope and mixed roofs, and a lot of apartment and condo buildings. Work gets phased so tenants keep access and parking, and low-slope sections get a membrane or coating rather than tile.

    Typically Downtown Glendale, Grandview, Riverside Rancho, Rancho San Rafael and the Grand Central / San Fernando Road corridor.
